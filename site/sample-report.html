In one paragraph

Sam started by saying they like coloring, and asked to make a coloring app. Most of the shape of the app came from Sam: pictures that run from easy (a snowman or a simple animal) to hard (a grown-up mandala), a palette for each picture, harder pictures that unlock as you finish easier ones, and the feel of a book you flip through. In Design, Sam picked a shelf of books for the first screen, each book labeled "Level #". A book gets pulled off the shelf and opens on page 1. The picture fills most of the screen with the palette beside it. Sam also asked for outline-only pictures with closed shapes, and for fills that happen right away and can be undone or changed. The guide filled in a few details itself: the name, 4 books with 3 pictures each, and a "Done" button. The guide said clearly that Sam could change any of those. The app was built and the session finished. While it was building, Sam came up with three more changes: a secret picture at the end of each book that shows up once the book is finished, a sparkle effect when it appears, and keeping the palette beside the picture on wide screens so the picture can run top to bottom. Once, Sam mentioned their school while saying who they wanted to show the app to; the guide hid the name, kindly said it isn't needed here, and carried on. Sam's answers were short and decisive, and the session went smoothly.

The app

🎨

My Coloring Book

A calm coloring app that opens on a shelf of level books. You pull a book off the shelf, flip through its outline pictures, and tap a color and then a shape to fill it in. Finishing the pictures in a book unlocks the next, harder book, and the app saves your progress on this device.
